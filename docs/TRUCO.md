# TRUCO — Official Rules

*The most fun card game ever played in a Brazilian bar. The rules read like they were written by drunk people, and that's the point.*

---

## 1. What You Need

- **4 players** (including you)
- **1 deck of cards**
- A table, ideally with people sitting around it laughing their heads off

**Deck prep:** Remove all the **8s, 9s, 10s, and Jokers**. You're left with a 40-card deck. Shuffle it well.

---

## 2. Setup

**Teams.** Split into **two teams of two**. Sit so that your **partner is directly across the table** from you — which means your two **opponents sit on either side** of you.

**The cut.** The dealer hands the deck to the player on their **left**, who *cuts* it (splits the deck in two and rejoins the halves), then hands it back.

**The deal.** The dealer gives **3 cards to each player**, face down. Nobody sees anyone else's cards — guard yours.

**The flip (a.k.a. the vira).** Before the **dealer or their partner look at their own cards**, the dealer draws one more card off the top and lays it **face-up in the middle of the table**. This is *the flip*. It does two things: it looks cool, and it decides the **Manilha** (see Section 5).

---

## 3. Card Ranking

From **worst to best**:

> **4 · 5 · 6 · 7 · Q · J · K · A · 2 · 3**

Yes, the 3 is the best regular card and the 4 is the worst. Suits **don't matter** for ranking normal cards — only for Manilhas (Section 5).

---

## 4. Objective & Scoring

| Achievement | How |
|---|---|
| **Win a round** | Play the highest card on the table |
| **Win a hand** (1 point) | Win **2 of the 3 rounds** |
| **Win a set** | Reach **12 points** |
| **Win the match** | Win **2 sets** |

A standard hand is worth **1 point** — but that number can climb fast once **Truco** gets called (Section 7).

---

## 5. How a Hand Is Played

Play goes **anti-clockwise**.

1. The player to the **right of the dealer** leads, placing one card in the middle.
2. Each player follows in turn, laying down one card.
3. The **highest card wins the round**. Whoever played the winning card **leads the next round**.
4. Win **2 rounds** and the hand (and its points) is yours.

**Strategy basics:** if your partner already has the round locked with a monster card, don't waste your good cards — throw your trash. If you need to win, you need to **beat the highest card currently on the table.**



> **Example.** The player on your right opens with a **2** (second-highest regular card). Your partner can't beat it, so he dumps his worst card — a **4**. Your other opponent sees his teammate is already winning, so he also dumps low — a **6**. Now it's you. You drop a **3**, the best regular card, and **take the round**. You won, so **you** lead the next one.

**Playing face-down.** Don't want anyone to know the card you are discarding? Play it **face-down**. Allowed **any time except Round 1**.
---

## 6. The Manilha (the wild card)

The **Manilha** is a special card that **beats everything else in the game.** It **changes every hand**, depending on the flip.

**How it's decided:** the Manilha is the card **one rank above the flip**, following the ranking order in Section 3 (and wrapping around from 3 back to 4).

| If the flip is… | The Manilha is… |
|---|---|
| 4 | 5 |
| 5 | 6 |
| 6 | 7 |
| 7 | Q |
| Q | J |
| J | K |
| K | A |
| A | 2 |
| 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 4 |

There are **four** Manilhas each hand (one per suit), and they outrank every normal card.

> **Example.** If the Manilha this hand is the **5**, and the cards played are an Ace, a 3, a 5, and a 7 — the **5 wins**, because a Manilha beats everything.

**Manilha vs. Manilha — suits break the tie.** When two Manilhas clash, the **suit** decides it. Suit order, **worst to best**:

> **♦️ · ♠️ · ♥️ · ♣️**

So if the flip is a 6 (Manilha = 7) and you play the **7♦️** while your opponent plays the **7♣️**, **he wins** — clubs beats diamonds.

> ⚠️ **Suits are a tiebreaker for Manilhas only.** With two equal *normal* cards, it's a **genuine tie** and **both teams win the round** (see Section 9). The Manilha of **clubs (♣️)** is the single strongest card in the game — it's called the **Zap**.

---

## 7. Truco (raising the stakes)

This is the heart of the game. At any point, if you're feeling it, you call **"TRUCO!"** — shout it, whisper it, sing it, whatever feels right. You're raising the value of the hand.

When Truco is called, the opposing team has **three options**:

- **Run.** They fold immediately and **lose the hand at its current value**.
- **Accept.** The hand is now worth **3 points**.
- **Re-raise.** They throw it back harder, climbing the ladder below.

**The raise ladder.** Each side can keep escalating: **Truco → Six → Nine → Twelve.**

| Call | Hand worth if accepted | You lose this much if you run |
|---|---|---|
| (no call) | 1 | — |
| **Truco** | 3 | 1 |
| **Six** | 6 | 3 |
| **Nine** | 9 | 6 |
| **Twelve** | 12 | 9 |

So if someone calls **Six** on you and you decline, you hand over **3 points**. Decline a **Twelve**, you give up **9**. The team that **runs** always loses at the **previous** value.

> 🎭 **Bluffing is the soul of Truco.** You can call Truco with garbage cards. Keep a straight face, sell it, and your opponents may run — handing you the hand. But if they call your bluff and **accept**… you're toast.

---

## 8. The Zap Slap

If the hand is worth **3 or more** (i.e. a Truco was accepted) and you **win it using the Zap** (the Manilha of clubs, ♣️), you get to **slap the card onto the forehead** of the player next to you. Do it **hard**. It's tradition.

---

## 9. Ties (and how to break them)

If two equal **normal** cards are the highest on the table, the round is a **tie** and **both teams win the round.** Resolve the hand like this:

- **Round 1 ties →** whoever wins **Round 2** wins the hand.
- **Round 2 ties →** whoever won **Round 1** wins the hand.
- **Rounds 1 & 2 both tie →** whoever wins **Round 3** wins the hand.
- **Round 3 ties →** whoever won **Round 1** wins the hand.
- **All three rounds tie →** nobody scores. Wash it and re-deal.

---

## 10. Hand of Eleven (the 11-point rule)

When a team climbs to **11 points** (one short of the set), a special rule kicks in:

- The two partners may **show each other their cards** and decide together whether to **play this hand** or **skip it.**
- If they **skip**, the **opposing team scores 1 point.**
- **The word "truco" is forbidden** in this situation — in any form, in any language, even as a joke. **Say it and you lose the entire game.**

---

## 11. House Rules (keep all of these — they're the fun part)

**Signals with your partner.** You're allowed to **secretly signal** your cards to your partner — a wink, a touch of the hair, a raised eyebrow. Develop a code. Just don't let the other team crack it.

**Cheating is legal.** Yes, really — it's literally a rule. Slip a second deck into your pocket, discreetly stack your hand, do whatever you like. **The only catch: don't get caught.** If an opponent spots you cheating, **you instantly lose the hand.** The one hard limit: you **cannot use outsiders** — no roping in a non-player to peek at your opponents' cards.

**Wrong-passing penalty.** Pass the cards to the **wrong player** at any point, and the wronged player may **slap your hand** — aggressively. House rule, fully enforced.

**Best enjoyed responsibly... or not.** This is a bar game. The rules genuinely play better after a couple of drinks, which is almost certainly how they came to exist.

## Quick Reference

**Card order (worst → best):** 4 · 5 · 6 · 7 · Q · J · K · A · 2 · 3
**Manilha:** the card one rank *above* the flip — beats everything
**Manilha suit order (worst → best):** ♦️ · ♠️ · ♥️ · ♣️  (clubs = the Zap)
**Raise ladder:** Truco (3) → Six (6) → Nine (9) → Twelve (12)
**Win:** 2 rounds = a hand · 12 points = a set · 2 sets = the match
